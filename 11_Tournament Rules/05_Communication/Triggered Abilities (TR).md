# Accountable Player
The **Accountable Player** is the player responsible for putting a [[Triggered Ability]] on [[The Chain]], as defined by the *Core Rules*.

The **accountable player** for triggers sourced from [[Battlefield|Battlefields]] depends on [[Control]] of the battlefield and is not automatically the player who brought the battlefield to the game.

Players are not required to notify other players of [[Triggered Ability|Triggered Abilities]] when they are not the accountable player, but may if they wish. At [[Low OPL]], a player is not required to notify other players of [[Triggered Ability|Triggered Abilities]] when they are not the accountable player, but it is encouraged for sportsmanship and learning.
# Forgetting Triggers
The **accountable player** must acknowledge their triggers by the time they would have an observable impact on the game. **If they do not, they are forgotten.**
As part of acknowledging a trigger, the **accountable player** must communicate the trigger to all other players in an understandable and observable way.

>*Examples:* 
>- Verbally announcing the trigger.
>- Physically indicating the trigger. 
>- Other means of communication understood and agreed upon by all players in the game.

A trigger is considered **forgotten** once its point of observable impact has been reached and a new [[Game Action]] is taken without the **accountable player** acknowledging the trigger.

[[Player|Players]] ruled to be abusing this rule to **intentionally** forget *mandatory* triggers that they are the **accountable player** for will be penalized. (see [[Missed Trigger]])

For triggers that occur *“At the start of each player’s first [[Beginning Phase]]”*, the accountable player has the full duration of the turn in which the trigger occurs to acknowledge the trigger before it's considered **forgotten**, regardless of its observable impact.

A [[Triggered Ability]] that was **forgotten** never goes on [[The Chain]], but is still considered to have triggered for the purpose of non-optional *“First time”* or similarly restricted triggers.
# Examples of Observable Impact
- The trigger would change a point total.
- The trigger would change a rune total.
- The trigger would add a Buff token to a unit.
- The trigger would impact combat or other game procedures. A card that reads *“When you play a spell, give me +1 Might this turn.”* does NOT have an immediate observable impact. It has an observable impact once it’s Might would be relevant.
- A trigger would cause a player to draw or discard.
- A trigger would cause a game object to be exhausted or readied.
- A trigger would cause a unit to move.
- A player asks for public information that would be impacted by a trigger.
- A player would be required to make a choice of whether or not to place an optional trigger on the chain.
