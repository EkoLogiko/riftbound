**Instructions** are a type of text that appears on [[Spell]]s and within [[Ability]]. They describe actions to be performed by one or more players.
Instructions will self-describe when they are to be executed. If there is no timing described, they will execute during resolution

> *Example:* An instruction says “as you play me, kill a unit as an additional cost.” That instruction will execute as the card is being played. If the card is given [[Repeat]] and the Repeat cost is paid, this instruction will not execute because the Repeat execution does not happen until resolution of the spell, after this instruction has executed.

Instructions can usually be recognized by the way they are written. An instruction is written in the imperative mood, using the base form of a verb and omitting the subject unless necessary.

A single instruction is made up of a [[Game Action]] and its [[Complement]].

If a conditional clause appears in instructions prior to one or more clauses with multiple instructions, that condition applies to all istructions in subsequent clauses in the same sentence.

>*Example:* A spell reads in part “If you’ve gained a point this turn, draw 1, then discard 1.” In order for either of the instructions that follow the “If you’ve gained a point this turn,” conditional clause to execute, the condition must be true.
>*Example:* Rogue Assassin is a legend that reads in part “[Action][>] [E]: If it's your turn, move a friendly unit in a showdown to base and if I'm [Empowered], ready it.” The first conditional clause in the activated ability applies to all subsequent instructions in the same sentence. The second conditional clause, “if I’m [Empowered],” only applies to the instruction that immediately follows it.

Instructions do not include trigger conditions or the events or actions that a replacement effect defines as being eligible to be replaced.

>*Example:* An ability reads “When you play me, draw 1.” The trigger condition for this ability is not an instruction. “Draw 1,” is the only instruction included in the ability. 
>*Example:* An ability reads “If a unit would die, banish it instead.” The text “If a unit would die,” is not an instruction of the ability.
>*Example:* An ability reads “As I’m revealed from your Main Deck, Add [2].” The text “As I’m revealed from your Main Deck,” is not an instruction of the ability.

Instructions of a [[Spell|spell]] or [[Ability|ability]] may reference actions taken during [[Finalize|finalization]]. They will do so by checking whether "you" performed those game actions. These instructions check whether the game action was performed, not by whom. If another player gains control of the spell or ability after the game action was performed during [[Finalize|finalization]], it will still be considered true for the purposes of these instructions.

>*Example*: Rampage is a spell that reads “As you play this, you may pay [C] as an additional cost.” and “Choose a friendly unit and an enemy unit. If you paid the additional cost, give the friendly unit +2 [Might] this turn. They deal damage equal to their Mights to each other.” If another player gains control of Rampage, although they are not the one who paid the cost, the “if you paid the additional cost,” condition will still be fulfilled.
>*Example*: Back Off is a spell that reads in part “If you played this from your hand, draw 1.” On resolution, Back Off checks if it was played from hand. If another player gained control of Back Off, as long as the card was played from hand, the condition will be true and the controller of the spell will draw 1.
